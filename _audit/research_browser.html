HANDLER — Research Browser

Surveyed ~30 games across spy, narrative, strategy, tabletop, roguelike, and investigation genres. Companion docs: spy_game_mechanics_research.md, alternative_genre_mechanics.md, handler_audit.md. Sister artifact: handler_audit.html (the playthrough audit itself).
Frame. The audit identified four problems with the played session: it felt linear; choices didn't bite; outcomes were telegraphed; the world didn't react. The research surveyed games that solve each one, and pulled out the mechanic each one solved it with. Click a question to jump to its mechanic cluster.
Q1
Why does the outcome feel telegraphed?
Because every state axis is visible. The canon (Phantom Doctrine, Suzerain, Pathologic 2, Alpha Protocol) hides at least one axis and reveals it post-hoc.
Q2
Why don't choices bite?
Because losses aren't named or lateral. Darkest Dungeon, This War of Mine, XCOM all derive bite from per-turn scarcity, lateral cost on a different axis, and named persistent victims.
Q3
Why is failure unsatisfying?
Because failure terminates instead of redirecting. Disco Elysium, Wildermyth, Blades in the Dark all turn failure into a more-memorable scene than success.
Q4
Why doesn't the world react?
Because nothing fires that the player didn't trigger. Dishonored chaos, RimWorld AI Storyteller, CK3 pulse events all run on world state independently of the last input.

Q1 · Information Asymmetry & Hidden State

Hidden quality axes
Phantom Doctrine · Suzerain · Alpha Protocol
Every axis the player can see, they will optimize for. Every axis hidden becomes the source of surprise. Suzerain hides Alignment / Unrest / Opinion-of-X. Phantom Doctrine hides which agents are sleepers. Alpha Protocol hides whose dossier-completion gates which dialogue.
→ HANDLER translation
Add quality.visibility ∈ {public, sensed, hidden}. Public shows in HUD. Sensed shows fuzzy ("rising" / "high"). Hidden surfaces only via specific authorize actions ("Run Counter-Surveillance Sweep") or threshold-triggered events.
Source grading on intel items
NITE Team 4 · Le Carré tradecraft
Every intel item tagged with reliability (A=trusted source, F=unconfirmed). Players can choose to act on F-grade intel — sometimes it's a trap, sometimes it's the only lead. Texture sells the fiction; reliability becomes a resource.
→ HANDLER translation
Tag every dispatch fragment that mentions a fact with a source-grade. SoP memos cite their sources. Players learn to weight what the briefing says.
Unreliable narrators
Pathologic 2 · A Perfect Spy
"Every narrator is unreliable… we give heroes the right to make honest mistakes, forget, and even lie." Multiple POVs of the same events surface contradictions (Rashomon). The briefing itself may be wrong.
→ HANDLER translation
Authored "briefing-vs-reality divergence" event type: once or twice per mission, the SoP memo predicts X, the dispatch resolves Y. Engine-flagged so it's deliberate, not LLM drift.
Counterintel-as-resource
Invisible Inc · CK3
Learning what's hidden costs cycles or assets. So uncertainty itself is a resource you can spend down. The spend-vs-stay-blind decision is a meta-choice on top of the mission choice.
→ HANDLER translation
Add archetypes whose only effect is to flip a hidden quality from hidden → sensed or sensed → public. Costs cover or a cycle. Player chooses to know.
Alpha Protocol's invisible dossier gates
Alpha Protocol · Mass Effect (failure)
83% of Marburg's dossier with <60% of Parker's gates a special line that flips Marburg neutral; 80%+ of Parker's instead unlocks a line that gets Parker killed by Marburg. Players can't see the gate; they discover it on a second playthrough — and it's why they replay.
→ HANDLER translation
Hidden-quality thresholds gate option availability *in addition to* affecting outcomes. The drafter is told "this option is available because hidden_X > 6" — the player just sees the option appear.

Q2 · Choice that Matters (vs. the Telltale Problem)

Roadwarden's tier vocabulary
Roadwarden
Choices are tiered: Fluff (flavor) → Spice (small shift) → Floodgate (significant obstacle, sacrifice-to-bypass) → Burnt Bridge (permanent). "Inaction is action" — ignoring a burnt bridge still produces an epilogue change. Trust is built when players see one resolve permanently.
→ HANDLER translation
Tag each authorize-option effect_tier. UI shows tier as visual weight. Burnt-bridge picks fire a guaranteed closure dispatch within 2 cycles ("Bucharest courier service reports a body recovered from the canal — identification matches REDFIELD") confirming the consequence.
Lateral cost
XCOM · Darkest Dungeon · This War of Mine
Choosing A doesn't just *not give you* B; it *actively damages* C. XCOM panic spreads from the dead soldier to the next. Darkest Dungeon stress contagion. This War of Mine: feeding one survivor means another loses faith. The cost lands sideways.
→ HANDLER translation
Each option's effects includes at least one *non-obvious* side cost on a different axis (heat on a different asset, suspicion in a different faction, trauma accumulation).
Named, persistent victims
XCOM · This War of Mine · Le Carré
XCOM's Dave the Sniper. Sister Berenice. Le Carré's joes. Loss matters because the entity has a name, a dossier, a history with the player. This War of Mine measurably changed player behavior by renaming "Inventory" → "Our Things."
→ HANDLER translation
Force the LLM to refer to specific people by name and one personal detail when ops cost something. Renderer prompt: "If this op burned/hurt/killed someone, name them and reference one detail from their dossier."
Reigns' compound timing
Reigns · Reigns: Game of Thrones
Today's "yes" funds a festival; three rulers later your treasury craters and a new card fires referencing the festival. Consequences are deferred and chained through *actor reappearance*, not through "+1 quality" memory. The card character returns; the player remembers.
→ HANDLER translation
Already supported by the fired-event ledger and per-agent fired set. It's an authoring discipline, not an engine gap. Author 2-3 events per scenario whose when clauses reference earlier fired events and whose prose names the earlier choice back.
Citizen Sleeper's visible clocks toward authored outcomes
Citizen Sleeper · Blades in the Dark
Each NPC arc has a visible clock filling at its own rate. Ignore an NPC and their arc resolves *without you* — usually badly. The player sees the inevitability and chooses what to attend to. Random and authored coexist: the rolls are luck; the clocks are inevitability with a known endpoint.
→ HANDLER translation
First-class Clock type: name, segments, tick, fires_event_when_full. Replaces ad-hoc threshold patterns. Cleanest single feature you could add to the engine.

Q3 · Failure as Content (not as Termination)

Disco's parity rule
Disco Elysium
Failed Red checks open dialogue paths that produce the most-memorable moments in the game (the hat speech, "Raphaël Ambrosius Costeau"). The trick is parity: a failure must produce a scene of equivalent or greater narrative density than success. If failure feels like nothing happens, the player save-scums.
→ HANDLER translation
Every option needs an authored failure delta AND an authored failure prose hook. Add failure_lean_on: [...] next to lean_on. The renderer is told to use one OR the other depending on the resolved branch.
Wildermyth's injury-becomes-trait
Wildermyth
Failed defense → "your warrior loses an arm and gets a crow's-wing prosthetic from the Witch in the woods" — permanent character change, not death. Same Effects engine drives combat and storytelling.
→ HANDLER translation
Forced events with options that each set_trait differently. Author this as a recipe: "scar event." Lost cover doesn't have to mean lost agent — it can mean LYNX gains a paranoia trait that changes her voice from now on.
Blades' Position × Effect grid
Blades in the Dark · Brindlewood Bay
Before each roll: GM declares Position (Controlled/Risky/Desperate) and Effect (Limited/Standard/Great). After the roll: success-with-cost ticks a Consequence Clock. Partial successes outnumber clean wins; the world advances regardless. The grid is small (3×3); the outcome space is rich.
→ HANDLER translation
Two pre-resolution enums per option: position, effect. Outcome delta scales by both. Drafter prompt receives the pair as constraints — "this option resolves at Risky/Standard." Renderer adapts prose accordingly.
PbtA's 7-9 partial success
Apocalypse World · Powered by the Apocalypse family
2d6 roll: 10+ clean win, 7-9 "yes-but" (most common), 6- the GM "makes a move." There is no null outcome. Even total failure escalates the situation rather than rewinding it.
→ HANDLER translation
Mandatory partial-success for at least one option per cycle. Authored as: option resolves to one of three delta sets selected by a hidden roll, but one set is always "yes-but." Write all three; engine picks deterministically by quality threshold; LLM writes the picked branch's prose.
Inkle's "sustain failure as long as possible"
A Highland Song · 80 Days · Sorcery!
Jon Ingold: "sustain the experience of failing for as long as possible." A Highland Song models hypothermia/exhaustion without instant death. Sorcery! allows rewinds but penalizes them. The game wants the player to live through the long, dirty failure.
→ HANDLER translation
Defer terminal events. A blown cover doesn't have to end the mission immediately — it can produce a "running" mode where every option costs more and every dispatch acknowledges the spiral. Failure becomes a mission act, not a final screen.

Q4 · Reactive World (events the player didn't trigger)

Dishonored chaos system
Dishonored · Dishonored 2
"An ongoing aggregate through the game. A more or less background system that allows players to ignore it if they so choose." Higher chaos = more rats, more plague, darker NPC dialogue. Dishonored 2 evolved this: chaos depends on *who* you killed (informed by the Heart, which told you each NPC's story), not just *how many*.
→ HANDLER translation
A "Network Footprint" / "Disturbance" aggregate quality that silently rerolls which event-pool the next cycle draws from. High footprint = harder cycles, more reactive events, dispatches that mention "increased counterintel activity."
RimWorld's AI Storyteller
RimWorld
Three personalities (Cassandra/Phoebe/Randy) generate events keyed to colony progress. The genius: events are *thematically targeted* (rich colony → raids; isolated colony → wanderers) so they feel like the world reacting.
→ HANDLER translation
Pulse events that fire between cycles, conditioned on world state, independent of player choice. A faction acts. An asset has a personal crisis. News breaks. Author a small pool per scenario; engine picks based on current quality vector.
CK3 pulse events
Crusader Kings 3
Pulse events fire at random within intervals, but conditioned on world state. Designers: "events feel relevant, impactful and immersive in relation to the underlying simulation." Personality traits gate emotional outcomes — same event, different stress impact for Vengeful vs Patient.
→ HANDLER translation
Already supported via passive events. Add fire_at_intervals + per-agent traits (next card) to gate which events fire and which prose the renderer uses.
Off-screen state changes
The Witcher 3 · Outer Wilds (loops)
Village changes happen off-screen; player returns to find different bandits, different banners, different ambient dialogue. The transition is invisible; the consequence is undeniable.
→ HANDLER translation
On returning to a location/asset after N cycles away, show change. "Marseille bureau has a new chief. He's read your file." Authored as cycle-N-of-absence beats; engine fires when the player re-targets a long-ignored entity.

Bonus: Asset/Roster Mechanics from spy fiction + tabletop

Asset burnout
Le Carré · Slow Horses · The Americans
Real spy fiction: "the cost of espionage isn't just death, it's what you live with afterward." Slough House's joes are agents who failed once and got shelved (the bureaucratic cost). Deep cover ages you. Time-in-cover degrades the asset.
→ HANDLER translation
Per-asset fatigue quality. Each op +N. Above threshold: refuses missions, demands extraction, or breaks. Pair with a "bench" archetype — recover, but unavailable for N cycles.
Per-agent traits + injuries
CK3 · Battle Brothers · Wildermyth
Every brother has 0-2 randomly-rolled traits (Fat, Brave, Drunkard) that mod stats and gate dialogue/event reactions. Two-tier injuries: this-cycle (heals) vs scar (forever). A forever-injury is a forever-quality on that agent.
→ HANDLER translation
Per-agent traits: list[Trait] and injuries: list[Injury] (with temporary: bool). Drafter prompt threads these. KESTREL with vienna_scarred resolves a diversion order differently from KESTREL without.
Heist specialist roles
Payday · Monaco · Blades-as-heist
Each crew member has ONE thing they're the only one who can do (Hacker, Climber, Face). The roster isn't interchangeable — each name is a verb. Options that require that verb either need that agent in roster or fall back to a worse delta.
→ HANDLER translation
specialist_for(verb): list[str] on each agent. Archetypes can declare requires_specialist: verb. Without the specialist: option still appears but with worse effects and an authored "improvised" prose tag.
Per-agent stress + coping mechanisms
CK3 · Darkest Dungeon
Acting against trait → stress; stress maxes → mental break → forced-event picks a Coping Mechanism trait → new decision unlocked. Stress translates "you played against type" into mechanical narrative consequence. Darkest Dungeon: stressed hero steals loot.
→ HANDLER translation
Per-agent trauma quality. High trauma changes voice prompt (sentences fragment, returns to one sensory detail), can trigger agent_refuses events, and at threshold fires a forced break event whose options each set a trait.

Bonus: Surprise & Subverted Expectations

Re-contextualization events
Spec Ops: The Line · Inscryption · Pathologic 2
5-10 rare events whose only job is to reframe prior cycles ("the asset you trusted for 5 cycles has been Bureau the whole time — your last 3 dispatches just reached the wrong desk"). Breaks linearity *backward*.
→ HANDLER translation
Author a small pool of high-priority passive events whose effects rewrite quality interpretations rather than quality values. The dispatch reframes prior dispatches.
Inscryption's meta-twist
Inscryption · Hades
The game *changes shape* between acts. Player thinks they understand the system; the system itself becomes the puzzle. Hades: death isn't reset — Achilles updates the codex.
→ HANDLER translation
meta_state that survives mission boundaries. The fired ledger and quality snapshots seed the next scenario's initial state. Cycle 6 isn't always the end — a meta-quality can flip on completion and unlock a longer second arc with the same engine.
The known-unknown card
Cultist Simulator · Sunless Sea
Telegraph that *something* will happen ("you have 3 cycles before the operation is compromised"), but not *what*. Players experiment toward "aha moments when you understand the secret laws of the world."
→ HANDLER translation
SoP can carry an authored "ominous-line" field when opposition.investigation_progress > 6. Renderer is forbidden to explain it. Player feels the dread, doesn't get the resolution until it fires.

Bonus: Resource Tension

Per-X scarcity + lateral cost + named victim
XCOM · Darkest Dungeon · FTL · Frostpunk · This War of Mine
A tradeoff bites when three things stack: (1) per-cycle scarcity (action points, hero slots, laws-per-week), (2) lateral cost on a different axis from the one you optimized, (3) the cost lands on a *named* persistent entity you've already invested in.
→ HANDLER translation
Each authorize option spends a non-renewable resource (this asset, this favor, this window). 3-4 options/cycle should each consume different resources so there's no strict-dominant pick. Lateral cost per-option. Named victim per cost.
Frostpunk's Book of Laws
Frostpunk · 11 bit studios
Most laws have negative impact on hope or discontent. Child labor vs. child shelter. Designers spent enormous effort tuning so dilemmas were "not too subtle to notice or too exaggerated to become comical." The bite is calibration, not severity.
→ HANDLER translation
When authoring options, each one's downside should be visible enough that a player would not pick it without thinking, but not so visible that the choice resolves itself. Cap visible modifiers per option to ~2 (CK3 lesson).
Citizen Sleeper's dice-as-hand
Citizen Sleeper
Each cycle you roll N dice (N = your condition quality). Each action slot consumes one die; the value modifies success odds. You allocate the whole hand simultaneously. Decreasing N over time forces specialization — this run is about *one* arc.
→ HANDLER translation
Replace "pick 1 of 4 options" with "spend 2-3 dice across 4 option slots" — same engine, but each option carries min_die and quality_delta_scaled_by_die_value. Multi-axis decision in one cycle without losing determinism.

The Saint setting deserves special call-out

"Saint hearing confessions during a plague" is the most engine-aligned setting in the catalogue. It forces the LLM to author content the engine cannot pre-compute (the petitioner's lie is unique per playthrough, and must not reveal the underlying truth the engine knows) — which is exactly the bar the audit argues the LLM should be working at. The deterministic engine handles absolution mechanics; the LLM voices a petitioner whose truthfulness is hidden state. Two cycles later, a forced event reveals what they were hiding. That's content no static branch could pre-author convincingly.
