§1 The Two-Layer Model framing

HANDLER's engine is a graph. It just doesn't admit it. Hard nodes are the world's skeleton — pre-authored, engine-owned, never mutating shape mid-mission. Soft nodes are the world's flesh — LLM-proposed, engine-validated, mostly ephemeral, attached to hard nodes via typed edges.

The hard graph is what your lynx_exfil.yaml already contains — restructured as explicit nodes and edges instead of a flat qualities dict. The soft graph is new: a per-cycle layer of LLM-authored sensory details, minor characters, atmospheric edges, and intel facts that the engine validates against the hard graph and either retains for prose continuity or expires when unused.

The seam between them is the most important part of the design. HARD owns mechanics. SOFT owns texture. The engine enforces both contracts — and refuses any soft proposal that would contradict the hard graph's truth.

Hard Nodes Engine · Authored

The skeleton. Mechanical truth. Slow to change. Authored as YAML.

  • Cast members — LYNX, KESTREL, MUNTEANU, REDFIELD, MERIWETHER
  • Locations — REDFIELD's apartment, the eastern station, Piața Unirii, the safehouse on Strada Lipscani
  • Adjacencies — which districts border which, which trains connect what
  • Archetypes — the verbs (ACTIVATE_SLEEPER, RUN_EXFIL, etc.)
  • Forced events — burn_redfield, kestrel_sacrifice, kestrel_warns_lynx
  • Qualities with named resolution rules — cover, extraction_stage

Lifespan · entire mission · never mutates shape

Soft Nodes LLM · Validated

The flesh. Texture. Born and forgotten per cycle. Authored mid-stream.

  • Sensory details — "a coat that smells of wet horse — Belgrade in November"
  • Minor NPCs — Wojcik the Romanian-speaking Polish courier; REDFIELD's cousin in Constanța
  • Atmospheric edges — "tonight Piața Unirii has a wedding procession crossing it"
  • Intel facts — "REDFIELD's wife buys bread from the corner stall on Tuesdays"
  • Per-anchor budgets — capped count per hard node so the slice doesn't bloat
  • Mortality — expire if unreferenced within N cycles · persist if reused

Lifespan · 1 cycle by default · extends on reference

§3 The Validation Seam how the engine refuses contradictions

Every soft-node proposal from the LLM enters a four-stage gauntlet. Failures feed back to the LLM as retry constraints — exactly the pattern the drafter agent already uses today. The engine is the line between texture and lies.

The Handler's Desk

You are at a SCIF desk in London. Each hard cast member has a personnel card pinned to a real corkboard. Soft nodes get pinned next to them as the field reports come in — sticky notes the LLM writes, tacked to the relevant person. Ephemeral soft nodes visibly age: yellowed paper, faded ink, eventually struck through with a red pencil and left as a reminder of what almost mattered. Stale notes can be physically removed by the player to make room for fresh ones — the player participates in the slice's curation.

Why it works Soft nodes are physically pinned to their anchor. The continuity story tells itself visually. The engine's "ephemeral by default, persistent on reference" rule maps cleanly to "fresh tack vs. faded ink." Fits HANDLER's existing aesthetic almost without translation.
What it costs No spatial geography (you don't see Bucharest). Personnel-heavy missions read well; location-heavy scenarios read poorly. Risks feeling static if the corkboard never moves.

The Bucharest Map

Top-down stylised city. Hard nodes are map pins — agents, locations, REDFIELD's apartment, the eastern station. MUNTEANU's investigation_progress renders as a creeping red shadow that expands across districts each cycle. Soft nodes annotate the map with small floating notes near their anchor: a vendor's name at the Unirii pin, a wedding procession across a square, a courier route drawn as a dashed thread.

Why it works Geography becomes legible. MUNTEANU's reach is felt, not described. Forced events ("agents must not be photographed at the same square") gain spatial truth. Soft nodes function as the LLM's "writing in the margins of a real place."
What it costs Map art is a real commission. Cross-mission reuse is limited (a Bucharest map doesn't serve a Berlin scenario). Risks foregrounding tactics over fiction.

The Dossier

Each hard cast member has a manila folder. Pages contain the authored dossier text. Soft nodes get hand-written in the margins as marginalia — the handler's pencil additions to the official file. Tabs at the top switch between cast members. The authorize-an-option ritual happens by writing on the page itself (drag an action chip into the margin's "orders" area).

Why it works Marginalia is exactly the right metaphor: official text is hard, hand-written notes are soft. The handler's accumulating annotations are the slice. Fits dossier- heavy genres (spy, detective, casework) beautifully.
What it costs No spatial geography. No visible passing of time outside the page-flip. Risks being even more text-heavy than what you have now. Best paired with one of the other directions, not used alone.

The Cable Terminal

Pure text terminal aesthetic — the lightest possible visual layer, conceptually adjacent to your existing frontend. A right-hand sidebar shows the live graph state: hard quality summaries plus the active soft slice for the most recently referenced anchor. The body remains prose-first; the sidebar gives the player a reading hint as to what details are in play this cycle.

Why it works Almost free to build. Keeps HANDLER text-first. Soft graph becomes a quiet sidebar of "story is paying attention to:" tags. The slice is legible without art investment.
What it costs Doesn't fix the "badly written storybook" feeling on its own — it's the same medium, just better-organised. Best as a floor for the engine work, not the destination.
§6 Honest Tradeoffs when soft-graph extension helps · when it overcomplicates

+ When it helps

  • The world reads as specific. Two playthroughs of LYNX-EXFIL produce two different REDFIELDs (different smells, different stalls, different unfinished cables) without authoring two scenarios.
  • The LLM finally earns its keep. Continuity emerges from material the engine cannot pre-compute — exactly the bar the audit argued for.
  • Renderer prompts get richer context for free. The slice carries forward; dispatches stop repeating themselves.
  • Authoring effort multiplies. One hard graph supports many narrative skins. The vendor remembered KESTREL is a beat the writer didn't write.
  • Validation reuses existing infrastructure. The drafter's retry loop is the same pattern — the engine already knows how to refuse and feed back.

− When it overcomplicates

  • The hard graph isn't fun yet. Soft graph is a multiplier; multiplying zero is zero. Polish the hard graph first.
  • Per-cycle soft proposals balloon the prompt budget. Slice pruning and per-anchor caps are not optional; they're load-bearing.
  • Consistency check has limits — a soft node can pass each individual rule and still contradict the spirit of the scenario. Need spot-check tooling.
  • Players don't see the slice. Choose a UX direction that surfaces it, or the LLM's labour is invisible and the game still feels thin.
  • Small models (Gemma 4 E4B) hallucinate soft nodes that pass validation but feel off. May force a model upgrade or much tighter prose-constraint prompts.
  1. Ship hard graph first. Promote qualities to typed nodes; add explicit locations, position, and edges. Get the visual UX (Direction A or B) running off the hard graph alone. ≈ 2–3 weeks.
  2. Design the soft-graph hooks during the refactor. Reserve a soft_graph field on State. Decide the soft-node schema. Make sure renderer prompts can accept an optional soft slice. ≈ 2 hours of design.
  3. Defer soft-graph LLM extension until the hard-graph version is playable and you know where it falls thin. Then add it as Phase 2 — by then you'll have real evidence of where it matters most.
  4. Phase 3 is LLM-as-GM. Once events are graph patterns and soft nodes have proven their worth, the engine has the schema and validation to let the LLM propose new edges and new events within strict typed bounds. This is the endgame; don't reach for it early.
