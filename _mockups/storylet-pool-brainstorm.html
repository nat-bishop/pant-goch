Design Note · brainstorm
Storylet Pool
v.0.1 · 2026-05-13

Stop authoring missions.
Author a pool.

The game has no mission boundary. There is one ongoing life in one strange place, and a pool of ~120 storylets the engine draws from. Each day the engine offers 3–4 from whichever are eligible against current state. Killing someone subtracts their storylets from the pool forever. Earning a scar unlocks a dozen new ones.

Authoring is local: write one storylet at a time, ship it, never touch a global graph. The LLM helps fill the pool — proposes storylets given a brief + current state, drafts prose, suggests when: predicates. The story is the path you walk through the pool. No two playthroughs use the same 30%.

The handler engine already supports the substrate (qualities, when-predicates, fired ledger, opposed checks). The leap is: the storylet pool is the game, not one mission's worth of authored content.

§ 01

The daily loop, from the player's seat

What you actually see

Same verb as today: authorize one of three or four options. What changes is where they come from — the engine queries the pool, filters by current state, weights by recency / pressure / NPC presence, picks 3–4, and renders them as the day's options. Below: an illustrative day from a frontier-town setting.

Day 37 · Late autumn
3 options
The morning bell has rung at the assay office. Smoke from the foothills hasn't lifted in a week. Choose your day.
A.
Visit Doctor Iselin at the bone-pit.
You owe her an answer about the boy you brought down last spring. She has not forgotten.
NPC · Iselin · trust 4
B.
Ride east toward the smoke.
Three days out and three back, and you will miss the council vote. But something is burning that wasn't burning before.
Trip · 6 days · locks 3 storylets
C.
Sit with Magistrate Caul on the porch.
He will offer you a position. He will not offer twice.
One-shot · forced if Caul·patience < 2
Reputation · Steady · 4
Coin · 28
Scars · 2

Each option is one storylet drawn from the pool. The tag in author view shows the gate; in player view it's gone. The current forced mechanic still works — option C above would have forced: true with a when: on Caul's patience.

§ 02

Anatomy of a storylet

The unit of authoring

A storylet is the existing Action type plus a couple of new fields. It declares when it is eligible, what choosing it does, and what optional follow-ups the engine should queue. The author writes exactly one of these at a time and the file lives wherever. 

id: visit_iselin_about_boy
title: Visit Doctor Iselin at the bone-pit
tags: [iselin, past_debt, moral]

# Eligibility — the engine offers this only when all hold
when:
  all:
    - npc[iselin].alive == true
    - npc[iselin].trust >= 2
    - flag[boy_brought_down] == true
    - not EventFired(visit_iselin_about_boy)

# What the player chooses inside the scene (opposed check)
outcomes:
  - id: tell_truth
    when: choice == "tell_truth"
    effects:
      - npc[iselin].trust += 2
      - player.guilt -= 1
      - flag[iselin_knows] = true
    leans: [honest, unburdened]
  - id: lie
    when: choice == "lie"
    effects:
      - npc[iselin].trust -= 1
      - player.guilt += 2
      - flag[carrying_the_boy] = true
    leans: [cornered, unsettled]

# Optional — queue follow-up storylets to be drawn later
queues:
  - storylet: iselin_corners_you
    when: flag[carrying_the_boy] == true
    delay: 3..7   # show up some day in this window
  • One file, one storylet. No central manifest. The loader globs storylets/**/*.yaml and validates locally.
  • The pool is the union of all storylets. No "act 1 / act 2" structure. Pacing emerges from when: gates that depend on time, scars, trust, etc.
  • Queued follow-ups let an author write a 2-3 beat arc without a meta-graph: storylet A queues B (with its own gate + a fuzzy delay window) which queues C. The engine still respects eligibility — if state shifts, queued follow-ups simply never become eligible.
  • One-shot vs. recurring. Most storylets self-gate with not EventFired(self). A few — chores, encounters — omit that and can recur.
§ 03

State the storylets read from

Three buckets

Storylets gate on a small number of state buckets. Authoring is tractable because every storylet only references state that already exists; adding a new dimension is a deliberate act, not an accident.

Player

  • reputation 3
  • coin 28
  • guilt 2
  • nerve 5
  • scars ["coyote", "fever"]
  • knows_secret ["caul_drinks"]
  • inventory ["lockpick", "letter"]
Numeric qualities + sets. Sets are easier to gate on than counters; prefer them.

NPCs

  • iselin.alive true
  • iselin.trust 4
  • iselin.knows_about_boy false
  • caul.alive true
  • caul.patience 1
  • caul.offered_post false
  • marlowe.alive false
Per-NPC qualities live under npc[id].*. Death is one flag flip — all of marlowe's storylets drop from the pool.

World

  • day 37
  • season late_autumn
  • flag[boy_brought_down] true
  • flag[smoke_in_foothills] true
  • flag[council_voted] false
  • locked_storylets 12
Flags are unset by default. Once true, they only become false via an authored effect — making "X happened" durable and reasonable about.

All three buckets are already representable in the current qualities dict — we just convention npc[id].x as a namespaced key, and add a per-NPC .alive sentinel that every NPC-touching storylet must gate on. (Or: introduce a real NPC type. Open question §07.)

§ 04

Where the non-linearity actually lives

Two players, same setting, day 40

Non-linearity isn't "branching paths" in the choose-your-own sense — it's two players see different pools on the same day because their state differs. Below: same world, day 40, two playthroughs. The pool is the same ~120 storylets. The eligible subset diverges sharply.

Playthrough A
Iselin alive · trust 6 · guilt 1 · Caul alive · spared the rider
iselin_asks_you_to_stay when iselin.trust ≥ 5 and not flag[council_decided]
caul_offers_a_seat when caul.patience ≥ 1 and reputation ≥ 4
rider_returns_with_warning when flag[spared_rider] and day ≥ 35
iselin_funeral
caul_replacement_arrives
DIVERGE
Playthrough B
Iselin dead · Caul disgraced · killed the rider · guilt 6
iselin_funeral when iselin.alive == false and day < iselin.death_day + 10
caul_replacement_arrives when caul.disgraced and day ≥ 30
guilt_breaks_open when player.guilt ≥ 5
iselin_asks_you_to_stay
caul_offers_a_seat

The dead/struck-through items aren't removed from the file — they're authored once, eternal in the pool. They're simply ineligible against Playthrough B's state. Neither author nor engine has to remember "B is on the dark path"; the state remembers, and the gates do the rest.

The big win for authoring: you write iselin_funeral once. It's available to every playthrough where Iselin dies, regardless of how, when, or under what circumstances. The combinatorial explosion that kills branching narratives doesn't happen, because storylets compose over state instead of forking trees.

§ 05

What setting hosts the pool

Four candidates

The setting needs three things: a reason for you to stay (so storylets accumulate), a small named cast (≤ 12) so each NPC carries weight, and tonal room for both intimate and high-stakes storylets. Four candidates ranked by my read of fit:

A

The frontier town

You arrived for a reason that's slipping away. Now you live here.
Stay
Geographic isolation; the next town is a week's ride.
Cast
8–12 named — doctor, magistrate, deputy, drunk, rancher, etc.
Tone
Moral western. Decisions echo for months.
Why fit
Strongest social grammar of these four — duels, debts, oaths, alliances all map cleanly to qualities + scars.
B

The strange small city

A place where letters arrive that weren't sent and the cathedral has nine bells.
Stay
You can't leave easily; the trains don't always go where they say.
Cast
Larger (~15), loosely connected; some never meet each other.
Tone
Fallen London. Gothic, dreamlike, anything goes.
Why fit
Tonal latitude is enormous — storylets can be anything. Cost: harder to make any individual NPC matter.
C

The long voyage

A six-month sail, train across a continent, or caravan to the interior.
Stay
Trivially — you literally cannot leave the ship/train.
Cast
~8 fellow passengers/crew, fixed roster.
Tone
Hitchcock + Conrad. Forced intimacy, slow dread.
Why fit
Geography is solved (one corridor) and the cast is closed. Cost: limited tonal range — everything happens in the same handful of rooms.
D

The haunted estate

You inherited / were summoned to / cannot leave a great house in the country.
Stay
A binding — debt, illness, the will. You can't sell.
Cast
Small (~6) — staff, family, neighbour, the priest. Plus the house itself.
Tone
Gothic chamber piece. Mortality runs hot.
Why fit
Smallest cast = deepest NPCs. The house as a quasi-NPC is rich. Cost: pacing is hardest here — the world doesn't push on you.

My lean: A (frontier town). The genre comes with built-in social vocabulary (debts, oaths, scars, vengeance, alliances) that maps to qualities almost without translation, and the medium-size cast (8–12) is the sweet spot for "every NPC matters" without "I can only write 6 mini-novellas." But B is the strongest tonal sandbox if you want maximum authorial freedom.

§ 06

What authoring looks like, day-to-day

You + the LLM

The shipped pool starts small (~30 storylets) and grows. Your day-to-day rhythm:

  • 1. Play until you hit a dead spot. Day 22, no eligible storylets feel right. The engine still picks 3, but the seams show.
  • 2. Ask the LLM to propose 5 storylets that would fit this gap. Prompt: "Player state X. Recent storylets Y. Setting bible Z. Suggest 5 storylets, each as (title, one-line pitch, suggested when: predicate, suggested effects). Bias toward ones that touch NPCs that haven't shown up recently."
  • 3. Pick one, refine the gate, write the prose. Or let the LLM draft prose against your accepted gate.
  • 4. Drop it in the pool. Continue. No graph update. No version bump. The next playthrough through that state finds it.

The LLM also helps with two recurring authoring problems:

  • Predicate suggestion. Given a storylet idea, "what would gate this naturally?" — the LLM is good at proposing 3-5 candidate when: clauses you can pick from.
  • Pool gap analysis. Periodically: "Run the current pool against 20 sample states. Where does the eligible set drop below 4? What state-shapes have the fewest storylets?" — this surfaces the unauthored corners of state-space.
§ 07

Open questions worth pushing on

Decisions to make before authoring

Pending

  1. How long is one playthrough? 50 days? 100? 250? Long enough that you care; short enough to replay. Lean: ~80 in-game days, ~3–5 real hours. Replay is the unit.
  2. Endings — one big arc, or many small ones? Does the pool include a small set of authored "ending storylets" gated on accumulated state? Or does the player just stop when they're done? Lean: 5–8 authored endings, each with a heavy when: predicate. Player still chooses when to authorize the ending storylet; multiple may be eligible.
  3. Time pressure inside the pool. Without mission deadlines, why is today urgent? Options: NPC schedules (Caul moves away on day 40 if not visited), seasonal pressure (winter forecloses options), accumulating decay (your nerve drops each idle day). Lean: a mix — seasonal gates + a handful of NPCs with hard timers.
  4. NPCs: real type or quality-namespacing? Introduce a first-class NPC authored type (with declared qualities, schedule, default storylets) or keep them as npc[id].* qualities by convention? Lean: first-class NPC type. Pays for itself the first time you want to enumerate "all NPCs at the saloon today."
  5. Does the opposed-check / leans system survive? It does — slot it inside any storylet's outcomes:. But most storylets won't need it. Pure flag-flip storylets are fine and probably dominate. Lean: keep it, use it sparingly. Maybe 20% of storylets have opposed checks.
  6. Log writer / Operations voice. Without "Operations" as a frame, what writes the running narrative? Options: silent (no log), first-person journal (your character), third-person omniscient ("She thought, that evening, about the bone-pit"). Lean: first-person journal. Cheapest tonal fit and keeps the existing conversation-based log architecture.
  7. How does the player explore the pool's reach? One concern: if 70% of the pool is invisible, players may never feel the design. Possible mitigations: post-run "you also could have…" reveals; subtle in-run gossip about events the player skipped. Lean: post-run reveal screen. Cheap and rewards understanding.
