— Revision Note · v.0.2 supersedes v.0.1 —

The previous draft proposed a free-text brief composer at the handler's desk. That verb has been retired. In playtest reasoning it failed three checks: the player could skip turns without consequence; the structured cost meters had to be bolted on after the fact; and the blank page invited paralysis where engagement was wanted.

This revision replaces composition with selection. Operations drafts the courses of action; the handler authorizes one. The verb is binary and forced. The LLM does more work, not less — it now drafts the options as well as the dispatches — and the player always knows what to do.

§01 — Premise

You don't write the words. You sign off.

You sit at a desk in a room without windows. Your agents are out there. You will never meet them. Their dispatches arrive between cycles — short prose, generated in voice, conditioned on what's actually happening in the field.

Every cycle, the Operations Desk reads the inbox and drafts three or four courses of action for your signature. Each option is a paragraph — what the agent would do, who would do it, what it would cost. You read them. You authorize one. That's the entire verb.

You aren't a writer. You're a senior who has earned the right to put a name to a course of action. Over ten cycles, the choices accrete. Some agents you push too hard. Some you don't push enough. Some you never authorize at all, and the Section Chief notices. The handler is the sum of the signatures on file.

— Operating Premise —

The handler is judged by the cumulative weight of his decisions, not by any single one.

A bold option may produce catastrophe or a miracle. A cautious option may produce nothing at all. The handler who learns the temperament of his agents — and of his desk — is the handler who survives. The handler who does not is replaced.

§02 — The Cycle

One verb. Authorized, again and again.

Every cycle is the same shape. Four stages, three of them belonging to the model, one to you.

§05 — Why the LLM

The model writes both sides of the page.

Revision 02 moves the LLM upstream of the player as well as downstream. Operations now drafts the options on the basis of the world state. The handler reads what the model proposes; he never writes from scratch.

An author can't write every option.

Each cycle's option-set is conditioned on the entire prior trace — the dispatches received, the agents currently warm, the Chief's mood, the asset's silence. A branching tree dies to combinatorial explosion by the fourth cycle. The LLM synthesizes options that name this cycle's facts.

An author can't write every dispatch.

Each dispatch is shaped by the option authorized, the agent who enacted it, the world state, and the agent's personality. ADLER's 231-word dry note and KESTREL's 487-word confession on the same nominal task are different texts. The LLM produces both in voice.

Personality colors what comes back.

When the handler authorizes "press KESTREL for the name," KESTREL is the one who decides what "press" meant. The agent's misreading of the option is content. Same authorization to ADLER would have been read as the instruction's exact letter. This is the killer thing the model does.

The same situation, twice, is a different game.

Operations doesn't draft the same four options the second time you run Op. Shepherd. The state differs by Cycle 3 even if the opening hand matches. Personalities persist; particulars don't. Replays are real plays, not the same path with different prose.

Validate the verb first. Strip everything else.

Revision 02's claim is that the authorize-one-of-four verb works naked. Before any exposure meter, before any confidence bar, before any campaign arc — does the player feel real agency from a single click on a paragraph?

This is the smallest fair test. If the verb passes, every other mechanic from the v.0.1 design (per-agent exposure, hypothesis confidence bars, ten-cycle arc) attaches cleanly to it. If it fails, we learn for cheap.

— Minimum Viable Test —
  • MissionOne. Op. Shepherd, abbreviated.
  • RosterTwo agents. ADLER + KESTREL.
  • CyclesFive. Verdict due cycle five.
  • VerbAuthorize one of 3–4 LLM-drafted options. No typing.
  • MetersNone. No exposure, no confidence bars, no political capital.
  • PassPlayer wants to know what Cycle 6 would have offered.
  • FailPlayer feels they are clicking through a Twine.
§07 — Engine Architecture

The spine survives. The model fills two slots.

The current Smallville engine — predicate-gated beats, authored effects, drift-tested encoder — carries forward almost unchanged. Two new LLM jobs slot into the cycle. The chat agent of the prior game is retired; the dispatch renderer takes its place.

What stays

Authored world state

Agents have authored profiles (skills, weaknesses, voice register). Missions have authored stages, outcome distributions, and named complications. Quality fields drive beat eligibility just as today.

Deterministic state writes

apply_literal_effects remains the only mutation path. Authorization of an option fires effects gated by the option's tags. Agent state — cover, availability, casualty — moves only through authored predicates.

Encoder & drift suite

The ASP encoder and engine consistency tests survive. Mission graphs are reachable-encoded. The encoder must continue to over-approximate, never under-approximate — same invariant.

What's new

LLM Job 1 · Option drafter

draft_options(world_snapshot, roster, recent_dispatches) → a list of 3–4 structured proposals. Each proposal has a deterministic tag set (agent, action verb, cost, return) and a free-prose paragraph. The engine validates tags against the schema; rejection → retry → fall back to authored boilerplate options.

LLM Job 2 · Dispatch renderer

render_dispatch(agent_voice, option, outcome, world_state) → 100–600 words in the agent's voice. Length, omissions, justifications are personality-driven. A downstream classifier extracts structured fields (cover_breach, new_facts, cost_overrun) and writes them through the spine.

One new tick trigger

cycle_close fires after the authorized option has resolved and the dispatch is rendered. Predicates re-evaluate. The Operations Desk begins drafting the next options. The handler returns to the desk.

A spy game made of reading — where the only thing your agents have to hold against you is what you authorized, and the only thing you have to hold against them is what they wrote back.
